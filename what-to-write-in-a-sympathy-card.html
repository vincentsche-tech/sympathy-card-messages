What to Write in a Sympathy Card

Facing a blank sympathy card is one of the hardest writing tasks there is. Here's what actually helps — 50+ heartfelt examples, a simple 5-step structure, and the etiquette that matters.

The Truth About Sympathy Cards

The people grieving don't expect you to be a poet. They expect you to show up. A short, sincere card with one personal line will mean more than a long, polished one that sounds like a template. The card itself is the message: I see your loss, and I care.

The 5-Step Structure That Always Works

1
Acknowledge the loss

Name it directly. "I was so sorry to hear about your mother." Naming the loss honors it.

2
Express your sorrow

"My heart goes out to you." One sincere line of sympathy is enough.

3
Share a memory or quality

The most powerful line in any card. "I'll never forget her laugh." If you can't, a genuine compliment works.

4
Offer specific help

"I can bring dinner on Tuesday" beats "let me know if you need anything." Specific offers get accepted.

5
Close warmly

"With love," "With deepest sympathy," "Thinking of you." Keep it simple.

Sympathy Card Etiquette: Do's and Don'ts

✅ Do

  • Send it — a late card is always better than none
  • Keep it handwritten if you can; a typed card is still thoughtful
  • Mention the person who died by name
  • Share one specific memory or quality
  • Offer concrete help with a specific time
  • Follow up in a few weeks — that's when support is needed most

❌ Don't

  • "He's in a better place" — unless you're certain of their beliefs
  • "Everything happens for a reason" — it rarely comforts
  • "At least she lived a long life" — it minimizes grief
  • "I know how you feel" — unless you've truly shared the same loss
  • Asking how the person died — let them share if they want to
  • Making it about your own loss — unless it's clearly welcomed

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best thing to write in a sympathy card?

The best cards acknowledge the loss directly, express sincere sorrow, include one personal memory, and offer specific help. "I was so sorry to hear about your mother — I'll never forget how she made everyone laugh. I can bring dinner on Tuesday" is a perfect card.

How long should a sympathy card message be?

Two to five sentences is ideal. Short and sincere beats long and generic every time.

Is "I'm sorry for your loss" okay?

Yes — it's simple, sincere, and always appropriate. Build on it with a personal touch if you can.

Should I send a sympathy card if I already sent a text?

Yes, absolutely. A text shows you're thinking of them; a card is a keepsake they can hold onto. Both matter.

What if I didn't know the person who died?

Focus on the person you do know: "I'm so sorry for your loss. I know how much your [mother/father] meant to you, and I'm thinking of you."
