How to Write a Sympathy Card That Truly Helps

The Golden Rules

  • Keep it sincere and specific. A short message from the heart beats a long, polished one.
  • Share one small memory — "I'll never forget how she laughed" means more than any generic phrase.
  • Offer concrete help. "I can bring dinner on Tuesday" beats "let me know if you need anything."
  • Follow up after the funeral. That's when support fades but is still needed most.

What to Avoid

  • ❌ "He's in a better place" — unless you know their beliefs
  • ❌ "Everything happens for a reason" — rarely comforts
  • ❌ "I know how you feel" — unless you've truly shared the same loss
  • ❌ Asking how the person died — let them share if they want to

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it okay to send a sympathy message by text?

Yes. A short, sincere text is far better than silence. If the loss is recent and close, a card or handwritten note means even more — but don't let perfectionism delay reaching out.

What should I write in a sympathy card?

Acknowledge the loss, express your sorrow, share a memory if you have one, and offer specific help. Keep it sincere — a few heartfelt lines are perfect.

Should I say "I'm sorry for your loss"?

Yes — it's simple, sincere, and always appropriate. You can build from there with a personal touch.

When should I send a sympathy card?

Within the first two weeks is ideal, but a late card is always better than none. Grief lasts long after the funeral.
